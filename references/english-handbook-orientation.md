# English curriculum handbook orientation

Secondary source basis: `語文領域-英語文課程手冊（定稿版）.pdf` supplied by the user. Treat the handbook as an interpretive/reference source and the formal curriculum as authoritative.

Use these notes only when they materially help a general review or a targeted curriculum claim.

## Interpretation principles relevant to assessment review

- Learning-performance items may recur across educational stages; recurrence does not mean identical demand. Difficulty and complexity can increase across stages.
- A star (*) marks a higher-level learning performance; a double circle (◎) indicates recurrence across stages. Do not treat these markings as ordinary text decoration or ignore them when the report explicitly cites a code.
- The handbook's learning-focus analysis expands each performance into more concrete behavioral meanings and pairs it with relevant learning content. These examples clarify scope but are not a requirement that every item reproduce the same surface task.
- English learning includes listening, speaking, reading, writing, integrated language use, strategy, culture, and thinking-related dimensions; do not reduce every item to grammar or vocabulary simply because those are visible in the stem.

## Assessment orientation

The handbook provides examples of varied assessment methods, including observation, performance/activity tasks, and paper-and-pencil assessment, and discusses differentiated teaching/assessment.

For this Skill:

- use the actual item to determine what is directly assessable;
- do not criticize a paper-and-pencil item merely because other assessment formats are also possible;
- judge whether the report correctly describes the language demand and whether the teaching recommendation follows from the evidence;
- when a concrete learning-performance/content code is cited, verify it against `curriculum-english-2018.pdf` and use this file only to clarify the intended meaning.

## Review cautions

- Do not assume repeated codes imply the same expected difficulty at Grades 3-4, 5-6, 7-9, and later stages.
- Do not treat a handbook example as a mandatory item format.
- If judging naturalness, pragmatics, accent, or advanced linguistic correctness would require specialist evidence beyond the supplied sources, mark it for subject-expert verification rather than overclaiming.
