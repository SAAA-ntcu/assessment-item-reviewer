# Teacher-reader readability rules

## Default reader

Default to the teacher of the target grade and subject.

Do not default to a mathematics teacher when the report is for another subject.

## Reader-centered test

For each substantive passage, ask whether the target teacher can quickly understand:

- what the item is asking;
- what the report claims about the response data/options;
- why that claim follows from the item/data;
- what teaching action is proposed;
- how the current paragraph connects to the next one.

## Comment framing

Prefer this order:

1. Reader problem: what the teacher may misunderstand, fail to verify, or struggle to connect.
2. Cause: what in the manuscript creates the problem.
3. Revision direction: what to clarify, separate, verify, or reorder.

Do not force the same lead-in phrase into every comment.

## Reading-load rules

Flag background or repetition when it materially obscures the immediate analytical or teaching line.

Common cases:

- long curriculum history before the actionable teaching point;
- repeated definitions already established earlier;
- several paragraphs that restate the same result without adding interpretation;
- terminology changes that force the reader to infer whether two labels mean the same thing;
- teaching recommendations that require the reader to reconstruct which analysis finding they address.

## Style restraint

Do not comment merely because another wording would be smoother.

Keep the author's professional register when the sentence is already clear, grammatical, precise, and usable.

For isolated typos or punctuation errors, a compact correction is enough; do not invent a long reader-impact explanation.
